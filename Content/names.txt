# Names for Rossville, Illinois - 1991.
# One directive per line: male | female | male-child | female-child | surname
#
# RETUNED TWICE OVER, on 2026-08-01. This pool used to be Ashcombe's: West Country English
# surnames for an English village in 1979. Both halves of that are now wrong. The town is
# Rossville, Vermilion County, Illinois - settled out of Kentucky, Ohio and Indiana, with an
# Irish minority that came for the railroad - and the year is 1991. A Cornish Trelawny in
# eastern Illinois reads as a mistake the way nothing else on the map does.
#
# THE "HEAVY LATER GERMAN LAYER" THAT USED TO BE ON THIS LINE IS GONE, corrected 2026-08-03,
# and the evidence was already in this repo. Denominations are a demographic fingerprint:
# German settlement in the nineteenth-century Midwest shows up in churches before it shows up
# anywhere else, as German Lutheran and German Catholic congregations, often with services in
# German. Rossville's four are Presbyterian (Scots-Irish), Methodist Episcopal (the frontier
# circuit-rider church), Christian/Disciples (Stone-Campbell, born on the Kentucky-Ohio
# frontier) and United Brethren (German in ORIGIN but English-speaking and widespread by then).
#
# NO LUTHERAN CHURCH. NO CATHOLIC PARISH. And not for want of Germans in the county - Danville,
# fifteen miles south, has both, including a German Catholic church holding services in German,
# and the county's five Catholic parishes are at Hoopeston, Danville twice, Westville and
# Georgetown. None in Rossville. The German population is real and it concentrated at the
# county seat, not here.
#
# A few German families in a town this size is fine and likely. A HEAVY layer would have built
# a Lutheran church, and there isn't one. See docs/research/WHO-LIVES-THERE.md.
#
# PETHICK, HALLORAN AND IVEY MUST STAY. Content/fixture-village.txt - Ashcombe, still parsed by the
# older editor tools - names its shop, its surgery and its garage after them, and
# particulars.txt quotes all three. They are the only survivors of the English pool, and all
# three pass as American anyway.
#
# THE STREETS ARE SURNAMES. Henderson, Gilbert, Stewart, McKibben, Holmes, Benton, Thompson,
# Harrison, Dale, Greenwood, Watson and Stufflebeam are all in this list on purpose: they are
# the town's own street names, and in an Illinois village that means they are the families who
# platted it. Stufflebeam especially - a real and distinctly local name, and a town with a
# Stufflebeam Street should have Stufflebeams living on it.
#
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# COHORTS. THIS IS NOW WIRED UP, and was the outstanding bug in the old file: it described
# `male-child` and `female-child`, shipped 51 of them, and NameTable.Parse silently ignored
# every one - so children were drawn from the adult pool and the village had seven-year-olds
# called Clarence. NameTable now reads them and falls back to the adult pool only if a child
# list is empty.
#
# A forename dates a person more precisely than anything else about them. In 1991:
#
#     the very old   born 1900-1926   Clarence, Opal, Orville, Bernice
#     older adults   born 1927-1945   Harold, Dorothy, Vernon, Wilma
#     middle-aged    born 1946-1965   Larry, Sharon, Dennis, Debra
#     young adults   born 1966-1973   Todd, Tammy, Shane, Kristi
#     the children   born 1974-1991   Jason, Jennifer, Dustin, Ashley     <- the -child lists
#
# The adult list deliberately spans all ninety years of that, because all of those people are
# alive at once and standing in the same post office queue. Only the children are separated
# out, because that is where the error was visible.
#
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# SIZING. Read this before adding or cutting.
#
# Surnames are the load-bearing pool. PopulationGenerator draws one per household and, for
# Sharers, one per member. Rossville village is 1,331 people, so about 600 household draws:
#
#     600 draws over  74 surnames  =  8.1 households per surname   absurd
#     600 draws over 380 surnames  =  1.6                          about right
#     600 draws over 900 surnames  =  0.7                          a town of strangers
#
# ABOUT TWO HOUSEHOLDS PER SURNAME IS CORRECT HERE, and is worth arriving at deliberately
# rather than drifting into. A village of thirteen hundred in eastern Illinois is not a town of
# strangers: there are three Millers on one street who are cousins and everybody knows which
# Miller you mean. Widening the pool to avoid repeats would buy statistical tidiness at the
# cost of the one social fact that matters most to a story about somebody who knows this town.
#
# Forenames are NOT sized that way and must not be. Repetition is the realism: 1991 rural
# Illinois ran on a few dozen names per sex, and a town where no two men share a first name is
# the artificial thing.
#
#     Real frequency is long-tailed - a dozen Roberts and one Kermit - and the generator draws
#     uniformly, so the tail here is flat. The content-only lever for that is to list a common
#     name more than once, since NameTable.Parse appends and a repeat is therefore a weight.
#     Nothing is repeated today, deliberately, so that a duplicate is always a mistake.
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
