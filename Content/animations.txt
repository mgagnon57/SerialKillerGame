# ============================================================================
#  WHAT PEOPLE ARE PLAYING
#
#  One row per situation. The left word is the situation; the rest of the line is
#  the clips that suit it, by their own names.
#
#  ONE ROW IS ONE LINE. There is no continuation character: the reader takes the
#  first word of a line as the situation and the whole of the rest as the clips,
#  so a row wrapped onto a second line silently becomes a situation named after
#  its first clip and is never looked up by anybody. The rows below are long and
#  they stay long.
#
#  ADDING AN ANIMATION IS A LINE HERE AND NOTHING ELSE. Drop the .fbx in
#  Assets/Noir/Animations - it configures its own import - add its name to a row,
#  and everybody in that situation can play it. No C#, no Animator graph, no
#  editor work. `Noir/Check The Animations` lists any clip in the folder that no
#  row mentions, so one that has been downloaded and forgotten says so out loud
#  rather than sitting there unused.
#
#  MORE THAN ONE CLIP TO A ROW IS THE POINT. Where a row names several, each
#  person is given one of them, chosen off their own citizen key - so a bar holds
#  people drinking in three different ways rather than a chorus line, and the same
#  person drinks the same way every time you look. A room where everybody moves
#  identically is the same failure as a street where everybody looks identical.
#
#  THE NAMES ARE MIXAMO'S OWN, unchanged, because the clip keeps its filename
#  through the import and the Animator state is called the same thing. There is
#  nothing to translate anywhere along that chain and so nothing to get wrong.
#  The two exceptions are `Walking Male` and `Walking Female`, which arrive from
#  the locomotion packs both called `walking.fbx` and cannot both be that on a
#  case-insensitive disk - they are renamed at the file, which keeps filename,
#  clip name and the name in this row all still the same thing.
#
#  A situation with no row, or a row naming a clip that has not been downloaded,
#  falls through to `default`. That is what makes a half-finished animation set
#  safe to run: people stand about rather than the city refusing to build.
#
#  A ROW IS A FLAT DRAW AND THAT IS THE THING TO WATCH WHEN ADDING TO ONE. Every
#  clip named gets roughly one person in N, so a row of twelve makes one home in
#  twelve into whatever the twelfth clip is. That is why `Piano Playing` and
#  `Guitar Playing` were downloaded and then deliberately NOT kept: they are fine
#  animations and one household in twelve at a piano is not a fine town. There is
#  no way to say "one person in this building does this and the rest do not", and
#  until there is, a clip that is only true of one person in a room does not go
#  on a row. `Bartending` is the exception that proves the shape of the rule - one
#  drinker in ten being the one behind the counter is about right.
# ============================================================================

# ---- moving --------------------------------------------------------------
# Movement beats activity. Somebody on the way to the pub is walking, not
# drinking: at fifty metres the eye reads the gait and never the errand.
#
# THE `m/s` IS THE SPEED THE CLIP WAS ANIMATED AT, and it is what stops the feet
# sliding. A Mixamo walk is a stride of a particular length at a particular rate;
# play it while moving the person at any other speed and the feet skate, which is
# the "gliding across the ground" fault exactly. Given the figure, the clip is
# played at the ratio between it and the ground actually covered, so at 1x the
# feet plant. Above about twice speed the clamp gives up and admits it - no clip
# can show a stride at 10x. Omit the figure on a row that is not locomotion.
#
# THE FIGURE IS PER ROW AND NOT PER CLIP, which is what decides who may share a
# row here. `Walking Male` and `Walking Female` were measured off their own root
# motion at 1.57 and 1.56 m/s before it was discarded, so the three walks agree
# to within a few percent and 1.5 is honest for all of them - the largest error
# left is about seven percent, which is a hundredth of a stride and invisible.
# The drunk and injured walks in the same download are NOT here for exactly this
# reason: they are much slower, they would skate against this figure, and one
# townsperson in four walking drunk at nine in the morning is the wrong town.
moving          1.5m/s  Walking, Walking Male, Walking Female
# Running is the child at the playground and NOBODY else. An adult jogging across
# Northgate reads as fleeing, which is a story event rather than a commute, and
# this game should not say that by accident.
hurrying        3.6m/s  Running
