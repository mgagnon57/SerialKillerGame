# Lessons learned — 2026-08-03

For review. Distinct from the other two: `POSTMORTEM` is what happened, `WORKING-BETTER` is the
rules going forward. **This is what we now know that we did not know on Monday** — each lesson with
the evidence that produced it and what it costs if ignored.

Some of these are worth more than the bug that produced them.

---

## A. About evidence

### A1. A sample window is part of the hypothesis, not a detail of it

**Evidence.** A cross-section of Holmes Avenue sampled ±14 m around the authored centreline. The
right-of-way it was looking for is 17 m away. Every sample landed inside the lot the road was
wrongly sitting on, so the reading was "the parcels tile through the streets" — the exact opposite
of the truth.

**Cost.** Two days, a correct finding retracted, an approved refit stopped.

**Apply.** Before believing a negative result, ask whether the instrument could have seen a positive
one. Widen the window until the answer changes or until the window is provably not the limit. A
scan that returns "nothing here" is a claim about the scan first and the world second.

### A2. All-pass and all-fail are the same fault

**Evidence.** `osm-tiger-data-is-not-a-survey` already recorded that county parcels tile farmland,
so "there are lots beside this line" is true in the middle of a cornfield — *a test where every
candidate agrees is broken*. The same trap was walked into with the sign flipped: 40 of 41 roads
"overrunning lots", read as a damning finding rather than as a broken instrument.

**Cost.** The note existed and did not fire, because it recorded one shape and not the symmetry.

**Apply.** Either extreme means the instrument is suspect. Write the note down symmetrically or it
only catches half the cases.

### A3. Measure against the unambiguous thing

**Evidence.** Road-versus-*parcel* was contaminated (parcels are tax boundaries and, as it turns
out, the roads were simply off them). Road-versus-*building* was clean the first time it was run:
75 road/building pairs, Chicago 47, alleys 27, streets 1. No interpretation needed — a house is a
house.

**Apply.** When a measurement needs a paragraph of caveats, look for a harder target. The clean
metric was available on day one and was not reached for until the owner described the symptom in
terms of houses.

### A4. "Fixed" requires a number that would differ if it were not

**Evidence.** Two claims of "landed" made from reading a diff. Both false — the second because
`CityStreets` has two tile-placement paths and only one was patched. The number that settled it:
*1111 alley tiles, median 4.0 m, previously 7.1*.

**Apply.** No adjective substitutes for a measurement taken after the change, in the artefact that
runs.

## B. About this codebase

### B1. Two numbers describing one thing will drift, silently

**Evidence.** `RoadClasses.CorridorWidth` (what the simulation reasons about) and the prefab's own
mesh width (what is drawn) were independent. They agreed by luck for the two city tiles and
disagreed by 77% for alleys and 29% for tracks. Narrowing the corridor changed nothing visible,
because the corridor was never the thing on screen.

**Cost.** A whole afternoon aimed at the wrong number.

**Apply.** Now fixed for roads by measuring the prefab and scaling to the corridor.
**This class of split may exist elsewhere** — anywhere Core states a dimension and an asset also has
one. Worth an audit.

### B2. A content-only kind is invisible to an enum lookup

**Evidence.** `kind school2` existed so `CityBuildings` could pick the American school prefab. Kinds
the `PlaceKind` enum has never heard of are numbered past it, and `DayPlanner` asks for
`PlaceKind.School` by member — so Rossville's 165 children had no school and never went, for months,
with the building standing there.

**Latent, and now pinned by a test:** `precinct` and `hospital` are `catchment yes` and content-only,
in exactly the same state. Harmless *today* only because nothing looks them up. The test fails the
day a third appears or one of those gains a caller.

**Apply.** Anything looked up by enum member must have an enum member. This is the second time this
exact fault has been found in this project — `PlaceKind.Dwelling` and `apartment` was the first.

### B3. The netstandard gate cannot see half the project

**Evidence.** A Core enum named `Light` compiled perfectly against `tools/Noir.Core` — which has no
UnityEngine reference, so there is no second `Light` to collide with — and blocked play mode
entirely. `Terrain` bit the same way an hour later.

**Apply.** A green `dotnet test` is not evidence that Unity compiles. Avoid Core type names that
UnityEngine also uses: `Light`, `Terrain`, `Object`, `Random`, `Debug`, `Material`, `Space`,
`Bounds`, `Color`, `Camera`, `Input`.

### B4. Derived geometry inherits every error of its source

**Evidence.** Houses are placed at a setback from roads. The roads are off their right-of-way by up
to 25 m, so `202–206 Holmes Ave` stand *inside* the street right-of-way. The road is on the lots and
the lots are on the road.

**Consequence for the fix.** Moving the roads onto their strips would drive them through those
houses. The four steps have to be done together — strips, centrelines, lot rows, re-measure.

**Apply.** When A is derived from B, correcting B without re-deriving A makes the map worse, not
better. Ask what else is downstream before moving anything.

### B5. Nothing in the suite asserts anything spatial about the town

**Evidence.** Two days of a visibly broken town, and `dotnet test` was green throughout at 324/326.
The suite tests Core logic thoroughly and the *town's geometry* not at all.

**Apply.** The building-overlap measure should become a test. It is cheap, it has no false
positives, and it would have caught this on the day the alleys went in.

### B6. Research documents can carry era errors

**Evidence.** `THE-YEAR.md`'s daylight table was right to the minute on seven of eight rows and an
hour out on 21 March — computed with the post-2007 DST rule, in a game that ends in 2006.

**Apply.** Any dated figure taken from a modern tool needs its rule checked against the period.
Corrected in place, with a test pinning the discrepancy so nobody "fixes" it back.

## C. About the data behind Rossville

This is the part worth keeping longest — it is a source ruling, and the lack of one is what let the
argument run for two days.

| Source | What it can be trusted for | What it cannot |
|---|---|---|
| **County parcels** (794 polygons) | **Where the public land is.** Demonstrated: a clean 19.5–20 m strip on every street, 4.5 m on every alley. This is the authority for road *placement*. | Corroborating a road that is already drawn — they tile farmland, so proximity proves nothing. |
| **Building footprints** | Whether a road runs through something. Unambiguous, no caveats. | Where a road *should* be — only where it should not. |
| **OSM** | Street *positions*, corroborated to 3–7 m by `rossville-streets.json`. Alley *existence*, corroborated by the 1940 aerial. | Extents. Uncorroborated by anything. `tiger:reviewed=no` on the IL-1 way, and `tiger:county` says Iroquois when Rossville is Vermilion. |
| **1940 USDA aerial** | Alley existence and mid-block spacing. | Absolute position — georeferencing failed four times; only the frame scale (0.647 m/px) is confirmed. |
| **The owner** | Everything about shape, character and what the town is actually like. Correct on Route 1's curve against the OSM tags, and correct on the roads not lining up, on the first day and every day after. | — but see the postmortem §4.2: *"the data is wrong here"* was over-generalised into *"the data is unreliable"*, and that is what dismissed the parcels. |

**The ruling that should have existed on Monday:**

> The county parcels are the authority for where public land is. The 1940 aerial and OSM say what
> exists. Building footprints are the check. The owner is the authority on shape and character.

## D. About the collaboration

### D1. The instrument came before the insight

Every attempt to reason about road placement failed until the parcel overlay was built and a
straight-down view was rendered. One image settled what a day of measurement could not.

**The overlay was requested by the owner as a side item, mid-diagnosis.** It should have been the
first thing built, and neither of us asked for it.

### D2. The correction always came from the owner

Not once in this session did the assistant catch its own error. Every turn — the retraction, the
half-applied fix, the wrong window — was caught by the owner saying some version of *it has not
changed* or *just look*.

**That is a finding about the process, not a compliment.** It means the self-checks in place are not
load-bearing, and the only real check is a human looking at the screen. Until the building-overlap
test exists (B5), that stays true.

### D3. Reversals cost more than corrections

Three source rulings changed mid-problem. Each was individually defensible. Together they meant
there was never a fixed thing to measure against, and the parcels — the answer — were ruled out
early and not revisited for two days.

**A ruling that is wrong but stable is cheaper than a ruling that keeps moving.** It gets tested and
overturned by evidence. A moving one never gets tested at all.
