# Two landmarks from the 1906 sheet

Read off `sanborn/crop-1906-schools.png` and `crop-1906-tileworks.png`.

## The schools — two brick buildings, and one has a tower

By April 1906 Rossville has **two separate brick school buildings**, both two-storey, standing
apart on the same ground near School and Church streets.

**Rossville Public School**
- 2 storey, brick, irregular cruciform footprint
- *"2 steam boilers for heating, 5 Dixon dry closets"*
- Its own well alongside

**High School**
- 2 storey, brick, **with a TOWER** — annotated as such, drawn as a rounded projection
- 32 ft across the main range
- *"Heat: steam. Light: elec."*

The 1930 county history (Williams) says a two-storey brick school house was built in **1868** and that
*"Rossville has always had excellent schools… matters in which the town takes just pride."* By
1906 that pride is two buildings.

**Why this matters for the build:** a **towered brick high school** is a silhouette. Alongside the
grain elevator, the water tower and the 1903 depot, it is one of the few things in this town that
reads from a distance. And the institution runs from 1868 to **2006**, when Rossville-Alvin High
School closed — nearly a century and a half, ending six years after the game's setting.

## The tile works — and what it says about the land

The brickworks that rebuilt the downtown after the 1893 fire, fifteen years on and under new
ownership.

| | 1898 | 1906 |
|---|---|---|
| name | **Habel Bros. Tile Works** | **W. B. Redden Tile Works** |
| brick | 25,000 / day | 25,000 / day |
| **tile** | **2,000 / day** | **8,000 / day** |
| plant | 2 kilns, brick kiln shed | 2 round kilns, dry house, engine & boiler room, office |
| safety | — | *"no watchman, no fire apps."* |
| supply | *"10 acres dirt available"* | *"dirt available"* |

Brick output is **unchanged**. Tile output has **quadrupled**.

That is not a business detail — it is the land being made farmable. Eastern Illinois prairie is
wet; it grows corn only once it is **drained**, and drainage means clay tile buried in the fields
by the mile. A works four blocks from the crossroads going from 2,000 to 8,000 tiles a day between
1898 and 1906 is the sound of the surrounding country being converted from prairie to cropland.

**So the town's own clay did two jobs**: brick for the main street after it burned, and tile for
the fields that paid for it.

---

## Confidence

The annotations are legible and quoted directly. The ownership change (Habel Bros. → W. B. Redden)
is certain from the two map labels; **why** it changed hands is not established. The tile works
does not appear on the 1913 sheets in the crops read so far — whether it closed, moved, or simply
sits outside those sheets has not been checked.
