# What Rossville sounds like

Compiled 2026-08-03. A village of twelve hundred is not quiet, it is *sparse* — long stretches of
almost nothing, punctuated by a very small number of sounds that are always the same ones. Getting
those few right matters more than layering ambience, and the commonest failure is silence: a town
with no trains, no insects and no machinery reads as a model rather than a place.

Confidence is marked throughout, in the same way the rest of this research marks it.

---

## The railroad — the metronome

**About fifteen trains a day.** The line through Rossville is CSX's **Woodland Subdivision**, and the
figure comes from the same subdivision where it enters Danville, fifteen miles south. Rossville sits
on it at **milepost 105.2**. Fifteen a day is roughly **one every hundred minutes, around the clock**
— which means a train is never far away, and the town's nights are punctuated rather than silent.
*Modern figure, and a Danville count rather than a Rossville one.*

**The horn is a fixed pattern: two long, one short, one long**, begun **15 to 20 seconds** before
each public crossing and not more than a quarter mile out. That is 49 CFR 222.

> **Era caveat, and it matters.** The federal Train Horn Rule post-dates the game's setting — it
> came in around 2005. In 2000 the sounding was governed by Illinois statute and the railroads' own
> operating rules, and the two-long-one-short-one-long pattern was already long-standing practice
> rather than federal law. **The sound is right for 2000; the citation is not.** Worth a check
> before anything leans on the exact timing.

Add the crossing itself: **bells and gates**, which start before the horn is audible and continue
after the train has passed. In a town this size, a single train is a sequence — distant horn, bells,
the horn twice more as it takes the next crossings, the train itself, then bells stopping.

**Passenger service ended in 1971**, so every one of these is freight. Long, slow, and at night.

## Cicadas — and one extraordinary year

**Annual cicadas every summer.** The "dog-day" cicadas run roughly **July into September**, loudest
in the afternoon heat, and they are the default sound of an Illinois summer. *General regional fact.*

**And then there is 2004.**

**Vermilion County is one of exactly four Illinois counties** — with Clark, Crawford and Edgar —
covered by **Brood X, the Great Eastern Brood**, a **17-year** periodical cicada. Its emergences:
**1987, 2004, 2021.**

So inside the game's 1995–2006 window there is **exactly one periodical cicada year, and it is
2004** — May into June, and eastern Illinois populations that year were reported as **among the
densest ever recorded**.

Which puts two things in the same twelve months:

| 27 February 2004 | fire takes a quarter of the downtown |
| May–June 2004 | the loudest natural event this county experiences, once every seventeen years |

That is not a coincidence anybody invented — it is what the record says happened. A player who is
in town in 2004 walks through a burnt block under a noise that has not been heard since 1987 and
will not be heard again until 2021. *Well sourced: Illinois Extension and regional press agree on
the counties and the years.*

## Harvest, which is the loudest season

**Grain dryers run through October, and they run at night.** Measured emissions sit between about
**76 and 106 dBA**, and dryers are documented as a genuine nuisance in rural communities precisely
because they operate overnight when everything else has stopped. Rossville has grain storage in
town, beside a dwelling, with no zoning buffer — see `photos/README.md`.

So **October is the one month with a continuous industrial sound over the town**, day and night,
for weeks. It stops when the crop is in. *Noise figures sourced; the specific Rossville dryer's
schedule is not.*

Around it: combines and grain trucks on the roads from late September to mid-November, tractors in
spring, and — because harvest runs into the shortening half of the year — **machinery moving on the
roads after dark**.

## The year in sound, otherwise

| when | what | confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mar–May | **chorus frogs and spring peepers** in the ditches and low ground, deafening at dusk | regional, general |
| Apr–Jul | **red-winged blackbirds** over the fields; killdeer on bare ground | regional, general |
| Jul–Sep | annual cicadas, afternoon | regional, general |
| Oct | **grain dryers, continuous** | sourced |
| year-round | **freight, ~15/day, horn at every crossing** | sourced (modern figure) |
| night, year-round | **coyotes** | regional, general |
| Dec–Mar | wind, and very little else — bare fields carry it | inference |

**Winter is the quiet one, and it is quiet in a specific way**: no insects, no frogs, no machinery,
leafless trees, and a third of the year of bare ground with nothing to break the wind. The trains
carry further in cold air.

## Two things I could not confirm

**The fire siren.** Rossville runs a **volunteer** fire department, and volunteer departments are
summoned by siren — which in a town this size is a sound the entire village hears and recognises,
several times a month. Many small Illinois towns also sound a **noon whistle** and test a tornado
siren weekly. **None of this is confirmed for Rossville specifically** — it is the ordinary pattern
for a town of this kind and it should be treated as likely rather than known. It is also exactly
the sort of detail the historical society could settle in one phone call.

**Church bells.** Four congregations are documented downtown historically. Whether any still rings
is unknown.

## What this changes in the build

1. **The train is the clock.** Roughly every hundred minutes, at any hour, and audible everywhere —
   horn, then bells, then the train. It is the single most characteristic sound the town has and it
   never stops.
2. **October has a floor of noise the rest of the year does not.**
3. **Seasonal insect and amphibian beds**, not one generic ambience: frogs in spring, cicadas in
   high summer, nothing in winter.
4. **2004 is a special case** and the game's window contains it.
