# Where each part of Rossville comes from

**Decided 2026-08-03.** One town, built as it stood around **2000**. But no single source describes
that town, so every element is taken from **whichever era documents it best** — and this file says
which, for each thing, so the next decision is not made by guess.

**The governing principle:** *take the fabric from whoever measured it, and the use from whoever
lived in it.*

A building's footprint, material and storey count were surveyed in 1913 and did not change by 2000
— those buildings stood until the 2004 fire. What a shop *sold* changed completely. So the shape
comes from 1913 and the sign over the door comes from 2000.

## What this rules OUT, explicitly

These are the mistakes the doctrine exists to prevent:

- ❌ **Do not build the 1913 town.** No opera house, no cinema, no steam laundry, no blacksmith, no
  shoe factory, no wagon works, no tile works, no three competing grain elevators. All gone by 2000.
- ❌ **Do not build the 2026 town.** No Casey's on the burnt corner — that fire has not happened.
  The downtown row is **whole**.
- ❌ **Do not use 1913 density.** A town that is 5-houses-in-8-lots reads as 1913, not 2000. The
  empty lots were filled by bungalows.
- ❌ **Do not use modern footprints for downtown.** There are only 19 in OSM and they postdate the
  fire that removed a quarter of the row.

## The one place the sources genuinely conflict

**How much of the 1913 downtown was still standing in 2000?** The 1893-fire brick row largely
survived — that is well attested, and the 2004 fire destroying "a quarter of the downtown
commercial section" implies three quarters were still there to burn. But *which* quarter went, and
what had already been lost or altered between 1913 and 2000, is not established.

**Working assumption, to be revisited:** build the full 1913 commercial row. It is the best
evidence available, it is certainly closer than generic blocks, and the error is bounded — at most
a quarter of it is wrong, and that quarter is the part that burned four years after the setting.

## A note on accuracy

Worth stating plainly because it is counter-intuitive: **1913 is better documented than 2000.**

For 1913 there is a professional survey giving every building's footprint, material, storey count
and use. For 2000 there is modern parcel data, 19 OSM footprints, aggregate census statistics, and
living memory. The historical record is the stronger one, which is exactly why the doctrine leans
on it for *fabric* and uses the modern record only where fabric is not the question.
