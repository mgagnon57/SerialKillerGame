# Downtown Rossville in 1991 — what the record supports

Dug 2026-08-04, for the 1991 build (see `THE-ERA.md`). **This page is deliberately thin.**
Almost everything written about Rossville's downtown was written *after* the February 2004
fire, and it mourns the block rather than naming what was in it. What follows is separated
into what is sourced, what is inferred, and what is still missing — the last list is the one
the owner can close.

---

## 1. Sourced

**The antique district is the downtown, and 1991 is the middle of its run.**
Rossville was called the *"Antique Capital of Illinois"* in the 1960s and 70s, with as many
as **seven Greyhound buses a day** bringing shoppers in. The trade ran roughly 25 years up
to the 2004 fire. So in 1991 the commercial row is antique galleries and gift shops — not
the 1913 Sanborn row of meat market, jeweller and drug store, which is a *drawing of the
buildings*, not a list of the trades in them.

**The fire: Friday 27 February 2004, about 7 p.m., the 100 block of South Chicago Street.**
It took a block-long run of buildings — *"a restaurant and gift and antique shops"* — about
a quarter of the downtown commercial section. Fire crews came from Rossville, Danville,
Hoopeston, Potomac, Watseka, Westville, Georgetown, Kickapoo, Lynch and Rankin. A Casey's
was built on the cleared ground afterwards, deliberately brick-faced to sit with the
surviving buildings. **None of that has happened in 1991** — the block is whole.

**The 1893 fire hit the opposite side** of the downtown from the 2004 one, and took about a
quarter of the commercial section as well.

### Businesses, with what is known about their dates

| business | address | trading in 1991? | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market Place Shoppes** | even side, 100 block S Chicago St | **YES — the one confirmed 1991 business** | Rosella Ray owned it **20 years**, sold it before the fire; the fire then destroyed that block |
| Raynbow Shop Antiques & Gifts | 111 S Chicago St | no | ~5 years, ending around 2006 |
| Twisted Sisters | 129 S Chicago St | no | opened November, 2006 or later |

**Market Place Shoppes is the find.** Twenty years of ownership ending shortly before 2004
puts its trading life across roughly 1980–2000, which covers 1991 squarely. It is the only
name so far that can be placed in the town in the target year.

## 2. CONFIRMED BY THE OWNER, 2026-08-04

He lived there; where he contradicts a source, he wins.

- **The WEST side of the 100 block of South Chicago Street burned.** This confirms the
  inference below - 111 and 129 are on the surviving side, and the Market Place Shoppes,
  "across the street" from 111, was on the side that went.
- **The restaurant that burned was at 121 E. ATTICA STREET** - not on South Chicago at all.
  So the fire wrapped the CORNER of Chicago and Attica, or ran along Attica too. Every
  source found says only "the 100 block of South Chicago Street", which is now known to be
  incomplete.
- **The Market Place Shoppes was a SINGLE SHOP**, not a multi-dealer mall, and sold
  something other than antiques. The plural in the name is decoration.
- **OPEN QUESTION.** His note reads "The West side burned 94" - if that means a SECOND fire
  in 1994, it matters enormously: 1994 is inside the 1991-2013 window but AFTER the build
  year, so in 1991 that side is still standing either way - but a 1994 fire would be a
  separate event from the 2004 one and is recorded nowhere in any source found. Ask.

## 3. Inferred — the chain the confirmation above rests on

**Which side of the 100 block burned.** Raynbow was at **111** and Twisted Sisters at
**129** — both odd, so both on the same side, and 129's building was bought intact in 2006.
The Market Place Shoppes was **"across the street"** from 111, so on the even side, and the
fire *"wiped out that block of buildings"* — the block the Market Place was in.

So: **the fire took the EVEN side of the 100 block of South Chicago Street, and the odd side
survived.**

That is a chain of three inferences off two addresses and one phrase. It is the single most
useful geographic fact for the 1991 downtown — it decides which side of the street the shops
that no longer exist were on — and it is exactly the sort of thing that should be checked by
somebody who was standing there. **Do not build on it until it is confirmed.**

## 4. Holes — what is still open

1. **What was in each unit in 1991.** The buildings are known from the Sanborn work; the
   occupancy is not. Being captured in the game itself - click a lot, type what was there -
   rather than gathered into this file by hand.
2. **The name of the restaurant at 121 E Attica.**
3. **Whether "94" means a second fire in 1994.** See §2.
4. **The 1991 trade names for the existing shops.** NOT a gap in the town - `Content/city.txt`
   already has the bank, the hardware, the grocer, the barber, the pubs, the filling station,
   the drug store, the bakery and the rest. They are named as 1913 TRADES, several of which
   cannot exist in 1991: a millinery, a confectionery, a general merchandise, a printing
   office. The slots are right and the signs over them are eighty years old.
