# The way Rossville is going

Compiled 2026-08-03. The game **opens in 1991** and runs forward. This file is the trajectory: what
changes, when, and in which direction — so the town can decline around the player instead of
sitting still.

**The short version:** the player arrives at the start of the worst decade the town has measured,
and watches every institution in view close.

---

## The population, and the shape of the fall

| census | people | change | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1980 | 1,363 | −4.0% | |
| **1990** | **1,334** | −2.1% | **the game opens here** |
| **2000** | **1,217** | **−8.8%** | the historic low, and the steepest single decade on record |
| 2010 | 1,331 | +9.4% | a rebound the player never sees |
| 2020 | 1,221 | −8.3% | |

**About 117 people leave across the game's first decade** — roughly one person a month, or a
household every two or three months. It is not a collapse and should not look like one. Nothing
dramatic happens to any single street. The town simply gets quieter, one closed curtain at a time,
and the 1990s are measurably the worst of it.

Peak was **1,588 in 1920**, so the opening town is already a quarter down from its high-water mark
and has been drifting for seventy years.

**Six blows in eleven years, and four of them land in the last three.** A character in 1991 has a
future; the same character in 2005 has had recent, specific bad news from up and down Route 1 for a
decade.

**And the region is going the same way.** Vermilion County falls 88,257 → 83,919 across the 1990s.
Danville, the employment anchor, is flat at ~33,900 but has lost its foundry, a General Electric
plant and a Hyster forklift factory. Hoopeston loses a cannery and a plant. There is nowhere nearby
that is doing better.

*(One thing that does **not** close: the PepsiCo/Quaker Oats plant, one of the county's largest
employers since 1969, runs through the entire window and beyond — it only shut in 2024. Depict it
as a going concern.)*

## The structural drift, which is slower and shows on the houses

These are not events; they are the direction everything is already moving in 1991.

**Household size is collapsing, so the town has more houses and fewer people.** In 1913 there were
268 dwellings for about 1,500 people — **5.6 per dwelling**. Today there are 517 improved
residential parcels for 1,217 — **2.35**. The town nearly doubled its houses while its population
fell. That is the ordinary twentieth-century story and it is most of why the place feels emptier
than the building count suggests.

**A third of the doors belong to somebody who is not behind them.** 239 of 794 parcels — **30.1%**
— are absentee-owned. In a village this size that is rentals and inherited property: houses whose
owners left and did not sell.

**The bottom of the housing stock is not being maintained.** A tenth of improved buildings are
assessed below **$4,361**, about $13,000 of market value. That is not a house anybody is keeping
up. The p10-to-p90 span is more than a factor of twelve.

**And it is ageing.** 20.1% of the population is over 65; the median age reaches ~42. A town losing
people to out-migration loses the young ones.

> **For the build:** if anything changes visibly over the fifteen years, it should be **houses
> getting shabbier and lots going vacant at the bottom of the distribution** — not uniformly, but
> concentrated in the worst tenth. `PARCEL-STATISTICS.md` already says 17% of residential lots are
> unbuilt; some of those became unbuilt during the window.

## What this means for `SOURCING.md`

The sourcing doctrine says *"one town, built as it stood around 2000."* For **fabric** that is fine
— the downtown row is whole from 1898 to 2004, so 1991 and 2000 are identical in brick, and the
house forms do not change.

**For use, they are not the same town**, and use is the half the doctrine says to take from whoever
lived in it:

- **In 1991 the antique trade is alive.** It is the town's identity and a real part of its economy.
  By 2000 eBay has bitten and it is going; by 2004 the fire ends it. The doctrine's *"antique shops,
  not harness repair"* is right, but it should be read as **the opening state, not the terminal
  one.**
- **The high school is open for the first fifteen years** of a story that runs to 2006.
- **The downtown row is whole for thirteen of them.**

The clean statement: **fabric from 1913, use from 1991, and both decaying forward on the calendar
above.**

## An honest gap

Everything here is dated at year granularity or better, but **nothing in this research establishes
what closed in Rossville itself during the 1990s.** The economy report searched for it and found
nothing: no named hardware store, bank, or grocery closure with a date. The regional blows are well
documented; the village-level ones are attested only in general terms — *"the village kind of
suffered through a period of decay in our buildings."*

That is the gap the local paper would fill, and the local paper is not digitised. See
`POLICE-AND-INCIDENT.md` — the Danville Public Library holds the Rossville microfilm, and a library
card gets what a script cannot.
