# The era — 1991 to 2013

**Set by the owner, 2026-08-04:** *"This is a 1991 - 2013 time frame. Make that a thing to
think about."*

## BUILD TO 1991. The rest of the window is later work.

Also his call, the same day: *"For now I want it as close to 1991 as possible. Add
deterioration later."*

So the window below is the eventual span, and **what gets built right now is the near end of
it**. One town, one year, 1991. Anything the twenty-two years would do to it — the fire, the
school closing, shops emptying, roofs going - is a LATER feature and is logged as one in
`docs/IDEAS.md` under City. Do not half-build it by ageing a few things now.

What building to 1991 settles, concretely:

- **The downtown block is WHOLE.** The February 2004 fire has not happened. Whatever stood
  on that quarter of the commercial row is standing.
- **The high school is OPEN.** Rossville-Alvin closes in 2006, fifteen years out. Both
  schools face each other across Chicago Street and both are in use.
- **The antique district is TRADING.** It ran roughly 1979 to 2004; 1991 is the middle of
  its run, not its decline.
- **Nothing on the map may date from after 1991.** A business that opened in 2006 is not in
  this town, however well documented it is.

This file is the one place that says so. Everywhere else in the repo that names a year is
downstream of this page, and several of them were wrong before it existed — the era had
drifted to *1995–2006* in `ROSSVILLE-HISTORY.md`, *2000* in `RESIDENTIAL-1913.md`, *late
1990s* in the economy report and *1991* in `Content/names.txt`. Four numbers for one
decision. If the window changes, it changes here and the others follow.

## What the window contains

Twenty-two years is not a snapshot, and that is the point — the town changes inside it, and
the change is the story:

| year | what happens | already sourced in |
|---|---|---|
| 1991 | the antique district is running; downtown is shops | `DOWNTOWN-1991.md` (to be written) |
| ~1996 | phone directories go online — see sourcing below | this page |
| **Feb 2004** | **the fire takes about a quarter of the downtown commercial block** | `ROSSVILLE-HISTORY.md` |
| 2006 | Rossville-Alvin High School closes; students bus to Bismarck-Henning | `rossville-economy.md` |
| 2013 | end of the window | — |

Two of those are not background. The **2004 fire** and the **2006 school closure** are the
kind of events that change what a town is, and both fall inside the window rather than
before it. A build set at either end of this range is a different Rossville: 1991 has a
downtown and a high school, 2013 has neither.

## The old research is history, not the setting

The Sanborn work — `LANDMARKS-1906.md`, `DOWNTOWN-1913.md`, `COMMERCIAL-ROW.md`,
`RESIDENTIAL-1913.md`, `BUILDING-CENSUS-1913.md` — is 1898/1906/1913. **It is not the era.**
The owner's instruction: *"those older research was just history of town and maybe an old
diagrams we could use."*

So read those files as what they are: the best surviving DRAWINGS of the town's fabric —
unit frontages, storey counts, where the brick is — from a period when somebody happened to
survey it properly. The buildings they measure are mostly still standing in 1991. The
TRADES in them are not; a 1913 meat market is a 1991 antique shop. Use the geometry, not the
occupancy.

## Where 1991–2013 material actually comes from

The window splits in half, and the halves are not equally reachable.

**1991 – 1995: paper only.** Nothing from these years is online in any form.

- 1991 tax rolls: **paper at the Vermilion County courthouse**, Danville. The county
  publishes online only back to 2007 (`Content/parcel-county.txt`).
- Phone books: the owner's own, or the historical society's.
- Rossville Historical & Genealogical Society, 108 W Attica St, 217-748-4080.
- Danville Public Library archives hold **microfilm for Rossville**, 217-477-5228.

**1996 – 2013: archived online.** Phone directories went online in 1996–97 and the Internet
Archive began crawling the same year:

| directory | online from |
|---|---|
| SuperPages (GTE yellow pages) | January 1996 |
| Switchboard.com | February 1996 |
| AnyWho (AT&T) | July 1997 |
| WhitePages.com | founded 1997 |

**This is the route to the pre-fire downtown.** An archived listing from 1999–2003 names the
antique shops while they are still trading — which no modern source does, because everything
written after February 2004 describes what was lost rather than naming it. The Wayback
Machine, the village's own early site, and the Illinois antique-trail directories are all
worth a pass.
