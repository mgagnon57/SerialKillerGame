# The downtown, read off the 1913 Sanborn sheet

Traced from `sanborn/rossville-1913-sheet2.jpg` (enlarged crop at
`sanborn/crop-downtown-1913.png`). Everything here is **read directly off a primary document** —
where a reading is uncertain the entry says so.

This is the four-block core Gilbert and Satterthwait platted in 1857 at the crossing of the Chicago
and Attica roads, as it stood after the 1893 fire rebuilt it in brick. **It is the model for the
game's downtown**, because the buildings drawn here are the ones that stood until February 2004 —
which is after the game's ~2000 setting.

**Colour on the sheet:** pink/red = **brick**, yellow = **frame**. The commercial row is solidly
brick; the yellow shapes behind it are sheds, ice houses, bake ovens and privies.

---

## The rhythm — the single most useful number here

Storefront widths are annotated on nearly every unit:

```
16'   18'   20'   22'   25'
```

That is **4.9m to 7.6m**, and it is the *beat of the whole street*. A downtown built from
uniform 10m blocks will not look like this. Most units are one or two storeys, with `2 st` and
`2d`/`2nd` marking where a second floor exists — and the second floor is nearly always something
different from the shop below it.

**Shops below, halls and offices above** is not decoration, it is the pattern, and it descends
directly from the very first building in town: the 1857 store Thomas Armstrong built with the Odd
Fellows owning the upper storey.

## What this means for the build

1. **Model the commercial core as a terrace, not as separate buildings.** Continuous party walls,
   varying unit widths on a 16–25 ft beat, one and two storeys mixed.
2. **The upper floors are their own thing** — lodge rooms, offices, a post office, a dentist. From
   the street that reads as taller windows and a different rhythm above the shopfronts.
3. **Two fraternal halls upstairs** — I.O.O.F. and G.A.R. — on opposite sides of Chicago Street.
   These are the town's social centre and they are *above shops*, which is the detail that makes a
   main street read as a real one.
4. **A cinema existed by 1913.** "Motion Pictures", 22 feet wide, on Attica.
5. **Service buildings go behind in frame**, not brick — tin shop, ice house, bake oven, sheds.
6. The four blocks are **small**. This is not a downtown that needs scale; it needs *density and
   variety* across a short frontage.

## Caveats

- **The house numbers on the sheet are not real addresses.** The map's own index says so:
  *"NOTE — House Numbers given are arbitrary."* Use the sequence, not the numbers.
- This is **1913, not 2000**. The buildings largely survived — that is the point — but the *uses*
  did not. By 2000 the row is antique shops, and the cinema, laundry, blacksmith, harness repair
  and shoe factory are long gone. **Model the fabric from 1913 and the uses from 2000.**
- A few readings are uncertain at this resolution: `B.B.S.`, `Confy D`, and one unit read as
  `Milly` (millinery). Marked rather than silently resolved.
