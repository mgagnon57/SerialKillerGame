# The downtown in 1898 and 1913, side by side

Traced from `sanborn/crop-downtown-1898.png` and `crop-downtown-1913.png`, both the same four
blocks at Attica × Chicago at the same scale. Fifteen years apart.

---

## The 1898 sheet documents the fire, on the map

Two labels, written by the surveyor:

> **"BEING BUILT"** — a unit on the west block, still going up in May 1898.
> **"RUINED BY FIRE"** — a frame building on the south edge of the same block.

The 1893 fire took about a quarter of the commercial section. **Five years later the map still
shows one building going up and one standing ruined.** That is not a secondary account of the
fire; it is the fire's aftermath recorded in the field by a man with a measuring chain.

Note which is which: the thing **being built** is in the brick row. The thing **ruined** is
**frame**. That is the 1893 rebuild in one image — brick replacing wood, exactly as the village's
own history says, with Habel Bros. Tile Works a few blocks away making 25,000 brick a day.

## What persisted, unchanged, across both

These are the load-bearing ones — a business that survives fifteen years is the street's spine:

- **Bank** with offices above — both maps, west block
- **I.O.O.F. Hall, 2nd floor** — the Odd Fellows, still upstairs, 41 and 56 years after the 1857
  building that started the pattern
- **G.A.R. Hall, 2nd floor** — the Civil War veterans
- **Rossville Shoe Factory**, with its own gas generator for lighting
- Drugs · Hardware · Groceries · Meat · Barber · Bakery · Millinery · General Merchandise
- Agricultural Implements and its shed, at the east end both times

## The finding that matters for the build

**The downtown was already a dense brick row by 1898.** The 1893 fire was rebuilt fast — within
five years the street is substantially the brick terrace it would still be a century later.

Between 1898 and 1913 the change is **infill and specialisation**, not expansion: the gaps close,
the frame survivors are replaced, and general stores give way to jewellers and tobacconists. The
*footprint* of the commercial core barely moves.

Which strengthens the sourcing doctrine considerably. If the row is stable from 1898 to 1913, and
the 1893-fire buildings stood until 2004, then **the commercial core was essentially unchanged for
over a century** — 1898 to 2004. Building it from the 1913 sheet for a ~2000 setting is not a
compromise; it is close to the best evidence that could exist.

## A caution

**"RUINED BY FIRE" in 1898 may not be the 1893 fire.** Five years is a long time to leave a ruin
on a main street. It could be a later, smaller fire the sources do not mention. The label is
certain; its cause is not, and this document does not claim otherwise.
