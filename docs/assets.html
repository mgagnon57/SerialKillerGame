Assets worth buying

Nothing here has been bought and nothing here is a commitment. The rejected half is the useful half — it is where the reasons are.

The four filters

Every suggestion has been through these, and they are why several obvious-looking assets are in the rejected list rather than the recommended one.

URP, Unity 6

The project is on Unity 6000.3 with the Universal Render Pipeline. An HDRP-only or Built-in-only asset is not a candidate whatever else it does.

Survives the chunker

CityChunker combines about 51,000 renderers into 5,200 and does it by destroying the originals. Anything needing a live script on a piece of scenery does not survive. Camera effects, audio and controllers are fine.

Doesn't replace ours

CityTraffic, LaneGraph, SunRig and CityDistrict are covered by the PlayMode suite. Buying over the top trades tested code for untested code.

Stylised, not photoreal

The whole city is mapped onto one flat-colour swatch atlas. A photoreal material next to that reads as a mistake, not as detail. What sells this world is light, weather, motion and sound.

Unity Starter Assets — ThirdPerson

free Free

The controllable person. The reason to start here rather than with a paid controller is measured: 78 of our 79 characters are already imported as Humanoid rigs, and Starter Assets drives any Humanoid. It should be walking around Northgate in an afternoon, and it brings a Cinemachine third-person camera with it.

Touches
A controllable agent alongside OrbitCamera, which already does Tab to street level and WASD. What's actually missing is a visible body, an animated walk and a camera that follows it.

Mixamo animations

free Free

The pack ships zero animations and zero animator controllers. It doesn't need to — a Humanoid rig retargets, so any clip from mixamo.com drops onto our people. That's why the paid animated-people packs are rejected below.

There is no bulk download — Adobe removed pack downloads years ago, so you pick clips one at a time. Fine, because the list is about a dozen rather than hundreds.

Settings on every download

You don't have to upload anything. Browse with Mixamo's own mannequin (X Bot or Y Bot) — Unity's Humanoid retargeting maps a clip onto our people whoever it was authored on, so the skeleton it was recorded against doesn't matter. Uploading one of ours only makes the preview match, and our characters arrive already rigged, which Mixamo's auto-rigger will try to redo.

If you want the preview to match anyway, upload one of the 79 under Assets/polyperfect/Poly Universal Pack/Meshes/People/ — note the SKM_ prefix, e.g. Slavic People/SKM_Man_Slavic_Summer_Hair.fbx.

In Place
Simulation decides where everybody is by pathfinding and AgentMeshView draws them there. A clip with root motion drives the transform itself, so animation and simulation would fight over the same number and people would walk off their own paths. Same reasoning as CityTraffic moving a car along its lane coordinate.

Asleep needs nothing — they're indoors, behind a wall, in the dark, and the only thing that shows it is the window not being lit.

Running is the child at play and nobody else. An adult jogging across Northgate reads as fleeing, which is a story event rather than a commute, and the game shouldn't say that by accident. AgentAnimation enforces it.

Into Unity

Drop the FBXs in Assets/Noir/Animations/, then per file:

  1. Rig tab → Animation Type Humanoid, Avatar Definition Create From This Model, Apply.
  2. Animation tab → tick Loop Time on the cycles; leave it off for one-shots.
  3. No materials to sort out, because you downloaded without skin.

Mecanim retargets through the Humanoid abstraction, so a clip authored on Mixamo's skeleton plays correctly on our people despite a different height and build. No scaling needed.

Know first
The player probably doesn't need Mixamo at all — Starter Assets ships its own locomotion. Mixamo is for the townspeople's activity states.
Gotcha
One of the 79 characters isn't Humanoid. It will silently fail to retarget until its import setting is changed — worth knowing so it reads as a settings problem rather than a broken clip.
Lands in
AgentAnimation.Drive, already wired and inert: it returns immediately on a null Animator, a missing controller, or a state that doesn't exist. The city keeps running while the set is half imported, which is what makes importing one clip at a time possible.

Atmosphere

The biggest gain per pound, because it works with the art style instead of against it.

HAZE — Volumetric Fog & Lighting for URP

~$40 Top pick

Light shafts under the street lamps. We have just replaced the cylinder-and-box lamp posts with the pack's own Lamp_Street_*, each carrying a real point light, and a wet street at 22:00 with cones of light down it is the single biggest mood gain available to this project.

Watch for
It composites with PostFx — check the two aren't both grading the image.

COZY: Stylized Weather 3

~$60 Recommended

Rain, snow, seasons — and explicitly stylised, which is why it sits above Enviro. Rain on the asphalt at night is most of the look for very little work.

Watch for
It wants to own time of day. SunRig already drives sun angle, fog colour and ambient off Sim.Clock, deliberately, so that how fast the day passes is a property of the game. Give COZY the weather and keep the clock ours.

Altos — Volumetric Clouds, Skybox & Weather

~$50 Consider

Volumetric clouds, which now have 270 metres of open country on every side to be seen over. Overlaps COZY on weather — pick one, not both.

Enviro 3 — Sky and Weather

~$80 Rejected

The most complete of the four, and the one I would not take: it wants to replace SunRig wholesale. SunRig is what lights the windows when somebody is home, glows the lamp lenses, and is wired to the simulation clock.

Revisit if
We ever want its whole system rather than a piece of it.

Audio

There is currently none at all — OVERNIGHT.md has said so since the beginning.

Footsteps Pack

~$30 Recommended

3,011 samples across concrete, grass, mud, gravel, water, metal and snow.

Nearly free
The map already knows the terrain of every tile, so world.Grid.TerrainAt picks the surface and the sample follows. Every other game has to author that mapping by hand.

Ambient Sounds — Interactive Soundscapes

~$45 Recommended

Global, 1D, 2D and 3D zones — which maps straight onto what the map already is: downtown, the suburb ring, the country frame, and the individual places inside them.

City Ambience Sound

~$20 Consider

Raw material for the zones above, if the soundscape tool needs feeding.

People

79 rigged humanoids, of which about 22 are in register for an ordinary town — ten Slavic, nine Steampunk, two Farm, four of the film crew.

PolyActors — Modular City People

~$50 Consider

Same publisher, so the style will match. The only people pack worth considering — and not for variety, which is already solved. Buy this if the Slavic and Steampunk register bothers you once there are figures on screen and you want ordinary modern clothes: suits, overalls, shop coats.

Nothing fixes
There is no elderly figure anywhere in the pack, so AgeBand expresses adult and child and nothing else whatever we buy.
A person's coat colour is a UV coordinate, not a texture. Universal_A_Alb is a labelled swatch grid — each row a role (primary, secondary, tertiary, hair, skin, hide), each row a ramp of about sixteen shades. Man_Slavic_Summer_Hair puts 2,841 vertices on 27 cells across 10 roles. Moving one along its own row recolours that garment and nothing else. Four roles at sixteen shades is 65,536 looks from one prefab, against a population of 365 — so no two people ever match, and it costs nothing.

Rejected, and why

The half worth reading twice.

Crowd & pedestrian systems

various Rejected

These simulate wandering. Our people don't wander — they have day plans and go to named places, and Agent.At is a PlaceId. A crowd system would fight the simulation rather than serve it. What we need is locomotion animation, which is free, and a NavMesh, which is built in.

Traffic systems

various Rejected

CityTraffic and LaneGraph are ours and are covered by the PlayMode suite. The give-way and junction-claim work landed on 31 July after four wrong theories. Replacing tested code with untested code is a bad trade at any price.

Low Poly Animated People

~$30 Rejected

120 rigged characters with animations, but 500–1,000 vertices against our ~2,800 — they won't stand next to the Universal people. And we don't need the animations: our rigs are Humanoid and Mixamo is free.

Low Poly Ultimate Pack

~$150 Rejected

Heavy overlap with Poly Universal, which we already have.

Photoreal environment & material packs

various Rejected

See the fourth filter. They would make this world look worse, not better.

If you only buy three

HAZE for the light under the new lamps, Footsteps Pack because the terrain data to drive it already exists, and COZY 3 for weather that doesn't fight the art style. Comfortably under $150 together, and every one drops in without authoring anything in a scene — which matters here, because content authored in an editor window is content MapAudit and the PlayMode tests cannot see.

And before any of it: Unity Starter Assets, free. Walk around Northgate at eye height with a real body first. Half of what looks worth buying from a list stops looking worth it once you've stood in the street.
